# Balance report

Generated by `npm run sim` (scripts/sim.ts): 840 games, 100 quarters each, 3 seeds per cell,
industries: saas, retail, mfg, logistics, energy, consulting, biotech. Took 27 s.

Each game is one scripted player against the rival AIs. Bots use only the actions a human has.
- **Passive**: Does nothing at all. The floor.
- **Steady**: Profitable growth, no outside money until it is cheap.
- **Blitz**: Venture-funded land grab: raise, hire, expand, worry later.
- **Premium**: Out-invest on R&D and charge for it.
- **Leveraged**: Debt-funded expansion; keeps all the equity.
- **Gouge**: Probe: maximum price, minimum staff, harvest cash.
- **Flipper**: Probe: raise every round offered and sit on the cash.
- **Warmonger**: Probe: sue and poach every rival, every chance it gets.
- **Raider**: Probe: borrow and raise to buy rivals outright.
- **Empire**: Probe: open in every city as fast as cash allows.

## Reading these numbers

- **The bots are a measuring instrument, not opponents.** Each plays one fixed
  strategy with simple rules. They read exact numbers every quarter but never adapt, so
  absolute win rates say more about the bots than about how hard the game feels to a
  person. What matters is the comparison: between difficulties, between strategies,
  and between honest play and the probes.
- **Difficulty is ordered.** The best honest strategy wins about half its games on
  Easy, a third on Normal, a quarter on Hard and under a fifth on Brutal. Doing nothing
  never wins on any level.
- **No degenerate strategy beats honest play.** Two exploits the harness found during
  development are fixed: compounding valuation by raising every round offered (Flipper),
  and wins handed out because rivals in thin-margin industries overspent on sales and
  collapsed on their own (which let Gouge win a third of Easy games). Gouge's remaining
  wins are biotech drug approvals, which is a legitimate route.

## Known gaps

**Industries differ a lot in difficulty.** Software wins 94% of bot games and
manufacturing none; logistics, energy and consulting are in single digits. The causes
differ:

- **Manufacturing, logistics and consulting** usually die of working capital: growth
  ties cash up in receivables and inventory faster than the bots can finance it. A
  person who uses the credit line, asset finance and pricing well does better.
- **Energy** almost never goes bankrupt but rarely wins inside 25 years, because every
  farm needs capital.

Next tuning steps, in order: give the bots working-capital awareness (draw the revolver
before a crunch, slow growth when receivables outrun cash); re-check manufacturing's
inventory policy (it produces to forecast and can overbuild stock); then, if the gap
persists, scale victory bars by industry growth potential.
